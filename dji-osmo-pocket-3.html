DJI OSMO Pocket 3

    Noah September 8, 2026

    October 28, 2025, afternoon – bought it at a physical store.
    I'm no pro, just a regular consumer shooting casually.

    Image quality: Video quality is noticeably better at first glance – that 1‑inch sensor really shows, and with the gimbal stabilization, phone footage can't even compare. It's way ahead of my Samsung and iPhone 12 in terms of video performance.

    Stabilization: Normal walking shake is basically invisible. Only when going up or down stairs do you catch a slight bounce – it's genuinely impressive, and this is exactly why the Pocket series exists.

    ActiveTrack: Works okay. It can lock onto a subject even in a crowd, but don't let the subject run too far off.

    Handling: Feels great in hand, very low learning curve. You can get going without even glancing at the manual.

    Battery life: I shot for maybe under an hour, about a dozen clips, and it drained around 50%. So a full charge should easily last two hours of normal shooting.

    Transfer speed: Pairing with the DJI MIMO app, file transfers are pretty snappy. A 2‑minute 1080p 60fps clip took about a minute to transfer – way faster than Sony's app.

    Heat: Gets noticeably warm while recording. When charging, the heat builds up quite a lot.

    Drawbacks

    • Aperture is fixed – can't change it, not even in Pro mode, not even for stills.

    • No intelligent noise reduction for audio.

    • Fixed 20mm lens.

    Buying advice
    Don't treat this as a camera replacement. With its 9.3‑megapixel effective resolution and design heavily skewed toward video (low pixel count for better dynamic range), the Pocket 3 just isn't a stills shooter – you'll get better photos out of most modern phones.
