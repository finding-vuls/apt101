    iPhone 18 Pro Might Be the Biggest Upgrade in Five Years

    The Dynamic Island is getting smaller. Apple's plan for the iPhone 18 Pro is to move the flood illuminator (the part that helps Face ID work in the dark) under the screen. Other Face ID components stay where they are. So yes, the Dynamic Island shrinks—but it's not going away completely.



    The A20 Pro chip will be built on TSMC's first-generation 2nm process. That's a big jump in chip technology.



    Apple's self-developed C2 modem is coming, along with satellite 5G support. This is Apple's third-generation modem, following the C1 and C1X. The C1 didn't support mmWave 5G. The C1X doubled speed and improved efficiency, but it still lagged behind Qualcomm in band coverage and compatibility. If the C2 finally brings mmWave 5G support—and even satellite 5G connectivity—that changes the game entirely.

    The main camera gets a 48MP Fusion sensor with adjustable aperture. Variable aperture isn't new on Android flagships, but the benefits are clear: a wide aperture lets in more light for cleaner night shots; a narrow aperture gives you deeper depth of field, so documents and group photos stay sharp without blurring.



    The iPhone 18 Pro Max is getting a bigger battery—finally crossing the 5000mAh mark. The trade-off is a thicker body, about 8.8mm.

    There's a new LTPO+ display. The name sounds like a small upgrade over LTPO, but if it can lower power consumption when the screen is static—on top of the current 120Hz ProMotion—that's a real boost to battery life.



    A new N2 chip replaces the N1, adding support for Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6.0. Wi-Fi 7 is theoretically four times faster than Wi-Fi 6E. Bluetooth 6.0 focuses on lower latency and better precision.

    The camera control button is getting simplified—it's losing the touch-sensitive layer and becoming a pressure-only button.

    Due to rising chip and storage costs, the iPhone 18 Pro is expected to start at $200 to $300 more than the current model.
